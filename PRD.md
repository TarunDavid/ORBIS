# Product Requirements Document (PRD)

**Product Name:** Offline AI Learning Platform (working title)
**Prepared for:** Team ALPHACENTURI — Smart India Hackathon 2026
**Problem Statement:** SIH26205 — Student Innovation, Theme: Smart Education
**Document Version:** 1.1
**Status:** Draft for development

---

## 1. Overview

This product is a fully offline, AI-powered learning application for school students (Grade-based, e.g. Grade 5 onward). It combines pre-loaded syllabus content (videos, notes, textbook PDFs) with an on-device AI system that can chat with students, answer spoken doubts, summarize lecture videos, generate flashcards, and create personalized quiz questions — all without needing an internet connection or any external API calls.

All AI models (chat, voice-to-text, text-to-speech) run locally on the student's device. Initial educational content is bundled into the app by the development team at build time. In addition, the platform supports local device-to-device content synchronization: an authorized teacher/school device can distribute new or updated educational content to nearby student devices over a local network without requiring internet access or cloud services. Student profile data, progress, and chat history remain local to the student's device by default.

The core value proposition: **a complete, syllabus-aware AI tutor that works anywhere, even with zero or unreliable internet access** — aimed at rural and low-connectivity students, per the SIH problem statement.

---

## 2. Goals and Objectives

- Provide students with structured, grade-appropriate learning content that works fully offline.
- Give students an AI study companion (chat + voice) that understands the specific chapter/video they are studying, not just generic knowledge.
- Reduce the student's dependency on internet access, data costs, and cloud AI services.
- Help students self-assess through AI-generated flashcards and quizzes tailored to what they just studied.
- Keep the experience simple enough for young students (Grade 5+) to use independently.
- Allow educational content to be updated and distributed locally without requiring every student device to have internet access.

### Success Metrics (for prototype/demo stage)
- A student can complete registration → reach dashboard → open a chapter → watch video → get an AI summary → ask a doubt via chat → ask a doubt via voice, entirely offline, in one smooth flow.
- AI chatbot/voice responses stay relevant to the specific chapter's content (not generic/unrelated answers).
- Quiz and flashcard content generated by the AI is relevant to the chapter the student just completed.
- App functions with zero internet connectivity from first launch onward.
- An authorized teacher/school device can discover nearby student devices and distribute a new or updated chapter/resource over a local network without internet access.
- Student devices download only missing or updated resources when synchronizing content.

## 3. Target Users

**Primary user: Student**
- School students from Grade 5 and above (dashboard and content vary by grade).
- May have limited or no internet access at home.
- Needs simple navigation, clear content structure, and a friendly AI tutor.

**Secondary stakeholder: Mentor**
- Referenced during registration (mentor name is captured).
- In this version, the mentor is recorded as metadata attached to the student profile; mentor-facing features (like progress dashboards) are out of scope for the initial version but noted as future scope.

**Content team: Developers**
- The development team acts as the "content publisher." All subject/chapter content (videos, notes, textbook PDFs) is prepared and bundled into the app before release. There is no in-app authoring or upload tool for end users.

## 4. Scope

### In Scope (Version 1)
- Student registration and profile creation.
- Grade-specific dashboard showing relevant subjects.
- Subject → Chapter → Content Page navigation.
- Content page with video playback, notes, and textbook PDF, styled like a video-platform layout.
- AI video summarization for the chapter's video.
- AI text chatbot for doubt-clearing, grounded in the specific chapter/video.
- AI voice assistant for spoken doubt-clearing, grounded in the specific chapter/video.
- AI-generated flashcards based on chapter content.
- AI-generated personalized quiz questions based on chapter content.
- Fully offline operation — no external API calls for AI, content, or data storage.
- Local device-to-device educational content synchronization over a local Wi-Fi / Wi-Fi Direct network, with optional Bluetooth support for lightweight discovery/control where appropriate.
- Versioned content manifests to identify new, missing, or changed resources.
- Integrity verification of synchronized files before replacing/installing local content.

### Out of Scope (Version 1)
- Mentor/parent/teacher-facing dashboards or progress reports.
- In-app authoring or general content-management tools for end users; authorized teacher/school devices may act as local content distribution sources.
- Cloud backup of student progress or chat history.
- Synchronization of student personal data between devices as part of the content-sync feature.
- Multiplayer or social/collaborative features.
- Support for content beyond what is bundled at build time.
- Payment, subscription, or monetization flows.

These out-of-scope items are candidates for future versions (see Section 12).

## 6. Core User Flows

### 6.1 Registration Flow
1. Student opens the app for the first time.
2. Student is shown a registration page and enters: Name, Age, School Name, Class/Grade, Mentor Name.
3. Student submits the form.
4. Profile is created and stored locally on the device.
5. Student is taken to the Dashboard.

On subsequent app launches, if a profile already exists locally, the student goes directly to the Dashboard (registration is a one-time step per device/profile).

### 6.2 Local Device-to-Device Content Synchronization Flow
1. An authorized teacher/school device prepares or receives the latest educational content package.
2. The teacher/school device starts a **Local Content Sync** session.
3. The device creates or joins a local network that does not require internet access, such as a local Wi-Fi network or supported Wi-Fi Direct group.
4. Nearby student devices open **Sync Content** and discover the available teacher/school content source without requiring students to enter an IP address manually.
5. The student device requests the current content manifest.
6. The manifest contains content identifiers, versions, file names, and integrity hashes.
7. The student device compares the received manifest with its locally installed content.
8. Only missing or newer/changed resources are selected for transfer; unchanged resources are skipped.
9. Files are downloaded to temporary storage and their integrity is verified using the expected hash before installation.
10. After successful verification, the new content replaces the older version atomically and the local manifest is updated.
11. Student profile data, progress, chat history, and other personal data are not transferred as part of this content synchronization process.
12. After synchronization, the student can open the newly updated content with the internet still unavailable.

### 6.3 Dashboard → Subject → Chapter → Content Flow
1. Dashboard greets the student by name ("Hello, [Name]").
2. Dashboard displays the list of subjects relevant to the student's registered grade.
   - Example (Grade 5): Maths, Science, Social Studies, Kannada, English, Hindi, Introduction to Computers.
3. Student taps a subject → sees a list of chapters for that subject and grade.
4. Student taps a chapter (e.g., Chapter 1) → lands on the Chapter Content Page.
5. Chapter Content Page shows:
   - The chapter's video, playing in a large player area (top of page, similar to a video-platform layout).
   - Below the video: chapter notes.
   - Below notes: the related textbook pages/section, viewable as a PDF.
6. On this same page, the student has three AI-powered actions available:
   - **Summarize this video** (AI-generated text summary of the video).
   - **AI Chatbot** (text-based doubt clearing).
   - **AI Voice Assistant** (spoken doubt clearing).

### 6.4 AI Summarization Flow
1. Student taps "Summarize this video."
2. The local AI model processes the video's associated transcript/description and chapter content.
3. A concise summary is displayed on the page.

### 6.5 AI Chatbot Flow (Doubt Clearing — Text)
1. Student opens the chatbot panel from the Chapter Content Page.
2. Student types a question about the chapter.
3. The AI answers using the specific video's description/transcript and the chapter's concept material as context, so answers stay relevant to what the student is currently studying.
4. Conversation continues in a normal chat format; context stays tied to the current chapter.

### 6.6 AI Voice Assistant Flow (Doubt Clearing — Spoken)
1. Student taps the voice assistant button on the Chapter Content Page.
2. Student speaks their question aloud.
3. Speech is converted to text locally (offline speech-to-text).
4. The AI generates a text answer using the same chapter-grounded context as the chatbot.
5. The answer is converted to speech locally (offline text-to-speech) and read aloud to the student, while also being shown as text.

### 6.7 Flashcard Generation Flow
1. After (or during) studying a chapter, the student can request flashcards.
2. The local AI generates flashcards (question/answer or term/definition pairs) based on the chapter's content.
3. Student can flip through flashcards to self-test.

### 6.8 Personalized Quiz Flow
1. Student requests a quiz for the chapter (or it is suggested after finishing chapter content).
2. The local AI generates quiz questions based on the chapter's content.
3. Student answers the questions within the app.
4. Student sees which answers were correct/incorrect, with the correct answers.

## 8. Functional Requirements

### 8.1 Registration & Profile
- FR1: The app must collect Name, Age, School Name, Class/Grade, and Mentor Name during first-time setup.
- FR2: The app must store this profile locally on the device (no server/cloud dependency).
- FR3: The app must use the stored Grade to determine which subjects appear on the Dashboard.
- FR4: The app must use the stored Name to personalize the Dashboard greeting.

### 8.2 Dashboard
- FR5: The Dashboard must dynamically display the correct subject list for the student's grade.
- FR6: Each subject must be tappable, leading to that subject's chapter list.

### 8.3 Subject & Chapter Navigation
- FR7: Each subject must list all chapters available for the student's grade, in order.
- FR8: Tapping a chapter must open the Chapter Content Page for that specific chapter.

### 8.4 Chapter Content Page
- FR9: The page must display the chapter's video in a primary player area.
- FR10: Below the video, the page must display the chapter's notes.
- FR11: Below the notes, the page must display the related textbook content as a viewable PDF.
- FR12: The layout should follow a familiar "video platform" pattern — main content (video) prominent at the top, supporting resources below, rather than a generic file-list layout.

### 8.5 AI Video Summarization
- FR13: The app must generate a text summary of the currently open chapter's video using the local AI model.
- FR14: The summary must be based on the specific video's content/description, not a generic chapter summary.

### 8.6 AI Chatbot
- FR15: The chatbot must answer student questions using the local AI model only (no external API calls).
- FR16: The chatbot must be "aware" of the current chapter — grounded in the video's description and the chapter's concept material — so its answers are contextually accurate.
- FR17: The chatbot must support a normal back-and-forth conversation within a chapter session.

### 8.7 AI Voice Assistant
- FR18: The app must convert the student's spoken question into text locally using offline speech-to-text.
- FR19: The app must generate an answer using the same chapter-grounded AI logic as the chatbot.
- FR20: The app must convert the generated answer into spoken audio locally using offline text-to-speech.
- FR21: The spoken answer should also be shown as text for accessibility and reinforcement.

### 8.8 Flashcard Generation
- FR22: The app must generate flashcards from a chapter's content using the local AI model.
- FR23: Flashcards must be viewable in a flip/reveal format.

### 8.9 Personalized Quiz Generation
- FR24: The app must generate quiz questions from a chapter's content using the local AI model.
- FR25: The app must let the student answer questions and see correct/incorrect results.
- FR26: Quiz difficulty/style may vary per student session to keep practice useful over repeated attempts.

### 8.10 Local Device-to-Device Content Synchronization
- FR27: The platform must allow an authorized teacher/school device to act as a local educational content distribution source.
- FR28: The teacher/school device must be able to start a local synchronization session without requiring internet access.
- FR29: Student devices must be able to discover an available authorized teacher/school content source over a supported local network.
- FR30: The sync service must expose a versioned content manifest describing available grades, subjects, chapters, resources, versions, and integrity hashes.
- FR31: The student device must compare the received manifest against locally installed content and request only missing or updated resources.
- FR32: Synchronized files must be downloaded to temporary storage and integrity-checked before being installed or replacing an existing resource.
- FR33: Failed or interrupted transfers must not corrupt the previously installed content.
- FR34: The synchronization process must update the local content manifest after successful installation.
- FR35: Content synchronization must not transfer student profiles, progress, chat history, or other student personal data.
- FR36: The student application must remain usable offline after synchronized content has been installed.

### 8.11 Content Packaging (Development-Side, Not End-User Facing)
- FR37: All subject/chapter content (videos, notes, textbook PDFs) must be prepared and initially bundled by the development team as part of the build process.
- FR38: The content structure must follow a consistent Grade → Subject → Chapter → Resources hierarchy so the app can reliably load the right materials.
- FR39: Each distributable content package/resource must have a stable identifier, version, and integrity hash so the synchronization engine can detect changes.

## 9. Non-Functional Requirements

- **Offline-First:** The app must be fully functional with zero internet connectivity after installation, including all AI features (chat, voice, summarization, flashcards, quizzes).
- **No External APIs:** All AI/ML processing (language model, speech-to-text, text-to-speech) must run on-device using local model files bundled with the app — no calls to cloud AI services.
- **Performance on Low-End Devices:** Since the target audience includes students with limited-connectivity/lower-spec devices, the AI models chosen should be lightweight enough to run responsively on modest hardware.
- **Data Privacy:** All student data (profile, progress, chat history) stays on the local device by default; nothing is transmitted externally.
- **Usability for Young Students:** UI must be simple and clear enough for a Grade 5 student to navigate independently, with minimal text-heavy instructions.
- **Reliability:** Video playback, PDF viewing, and AI responses should degrade gracefully (e.g., clear loading indicators) rather than crashing, especially on lower-end hardware.
- **Consistency Across Grades:** The dashboard and content structure logic must scale cleanly as more grades and subjects are added, without requiring app redesign.
- **Local Sync Reliability:** Content synchronization should tolerate interrupted transfers, verify integrity, and avoid corrupting already-installed resources.
- **Local-Network Efficiency:** Synchronization should minimize transferred data by skipping unchanged resources and, where practical, support resumable or chunked transfers for large files such as videos and PDFs.

---

## 10. AI/ML Behavior Requirements

- The local language model powers three features: video summarization, chatbot doubt-clearing, and voice assistant doubt-clearing.
- For summarization and doubt-clearing to be accurate, the AI must be given the relevant chapter's context (video description/transcript plus supporting notes) alongside the student's question — so its answers stay tied to what the student is currently studying rather than answering from general knowledge alone.
- Voice interaction requires two additional local components: speech-to-text (to understand the student's spoken question) and text-to-speech (to speak the answer back).
- Flashcard and quiz generation use the same local model, drawing only from the specific chapter's bundled content, so generated questions match what the student has just studied.
- All AI processing happens on-device; response times should be reasonable for an interactive conversation on the target hardware.

## 12. Local Content Synchronization Architecture

The local content synchronization subsystem is intentionally separated from the student-data and AI subsystems. Its purpose is to distribute educational resources, not to synchronize student records.

### 12.1 Logical Components
- **Teacher/School Content Source:** An authorized device holding the latest local content package and exposing a temporary local sync service.
- **Sync Manager:** Coordinates discovery, manifest comparison, file transfer, integrity verification, installation, and local manifest updates on student devices.
- **Transport Layer:** Supports a local Wi-Fi network as the initial implementation path, with Wi-Fi Direct as a device-to-device transport and Bluetooth reserved for lightweight discovery/control where appropriate.
- **Content Manifest:** Records content identifiers, versions, file names, sizes, and integrity hashes.
- **Local Content Store:** Stores installed videos, notes, PDFs, and metadata on the student's device.

### 12.2 Synchronization Strategy
The synchronization process is version-aware and differential: the student device first retrieves a manifest, compares versions/hashes with local content, and transfers only missing or changed resources. Large files should be transferred through a robust local network transport rather than relying on Bluetooth as the primary content-transfer mechanism.

### 12.3 Integrity and Failure Handling
Resources are written to temporary files during transfer. After the expected integrity hash is verified, the resource is promoted into the active content store and the local manifest is updated. Interrupted or failed transfers must leave the previously valid version untouched.

### 12.4 Privacy Boundary
The local synchronization subsystem transfers educational content only. Student profile information, progress, quiz history, and chat history remain on the student device by default and are not included in the content-sync protocol.

## 12. Data Entities (Conceptual, No Schema)

- **Student Profile:** Name, Age, School Name, Grade/Class, Mentor Name.
- **Grade:** A grade level (e.g., Grade 5), which determines the subject list shown.
- **Subject:** Belongs to a Grade; contains an ordered list of Chapters.
- **Chapter:** Belongs to a Subject; contains one Video, associated Notes, and a Textbook PDF section.
- **Resource:** The individual content items attached to a Chapter (video file, notes text, PDF file).
- **Chat Session:** A record of chatbot/voice interactions tied to a specific Chapter and Student.
- **Flashcard Set:** AI-generated flashcards tied to a specific Chapter.
- **Quiz Attempt:** AI-generated quiz questions and the student's answers/results, tied to a specific Chapter.
- **Progress:** Tracks which chapters/subjects a student has engaged with (for future personalization features).

## 14. Assumptions

- The dev team will source or create all video, notes, and textbook PDF content per grade/subject/chapter before release; content creation itself is outside this PRD's scope.
- "Mentor Name" is captured as profile metadata only in this version; no mentor login or mentor-facing view exists yet.
- The initial demo/prototype will likely cover one grade (e.g., Grade 5) fully, with the architecture designed to extend to other grades later.
- Target device specifications (minimum RAM/storage) will be defined by the team based on the chosen local AI model's footprint.
- The initial prototype may use a local Wi-Fi network for synchronization before introducing direct Wi-Fi Direct transport, provided the final architecture keeps the transport layer replaceable.
- An authorized teacher/school device is available when new educational content needs to be distributed locally.

## 15. Risks & Considerations

| Risk | Consideration |
|---|---|
| Local AI response quality/speed on low-end devices | Keep the language model small and quantized; test on representative low-spec hardware early |
| Voice recognition accuracy for young students' speech | Test Whisper's performance with children's voices and background noise typical of home/rural settings |
| Content storage size (videos + PDFs bundled in-app) | Plan storage/compression strategy so app install size stays reasonable |
| Keeping AI answers grounded to the right chapter | Ensure the chapter's context is reliably passed to the AI for every chat/voice/summarization request |
| Scaling content structure to more grades/subjects later | Keep the Grade → Subject → Chapter → Resource structure consistent from the start |
| Local sync reliability on heterogeneous devices | Use versioned manifests, integrity hashes, temporary files, failure-safe replacement, and a simple local-network transport first |
| Large content transfer over local networks | Skip unchanged files and use resumable/chunked transfer where practical; prioritize local Wi-Fi/Wi-Fi Direct over Bluetooth for large assets |
| Unauthorized content distribution | Restrict sync mode to an authorized teacher/school device and validate the expected content-source identity before accepting updates |

## 16. Future Scope (Post-Version 1)

- Support for additional grades and subjects beyond the initial demo set.
- Optional internet-assisted synchronization or centralized publishing when connectivity is available; the core local device-to-device content-sync path remains independent of internet access.

## 17. Summary

This PRD describes an offline-first learning app where a student registers once, then navigates a grade-specific dashboard into subjects and chapters. Each chapter's content page functions like a video platform: video on top, notes and textbook PDF below. From that same page, students can get an AI-generated summary of the video, chat with an AI tutor, or talk to a voice assistant — all powered by fully local AI models with no internet or external APIs involved. The same on-device AI also generates flashcards and personalized quizzes from chapter content, helping students self-assess as they learn.
